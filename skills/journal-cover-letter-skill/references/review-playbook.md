# Review and synthesis playbook

## Editorial purpose

Explain why the evidence needs synthesis, how the review reorganizes or interprets it, what new insight emerges, and how that insight changes research or practice. Search rigor establishes credibility but is rarely the lead contribution.

## Required story elements

1. Field problem or unresolved tension.
2. Evidence base and synthesis method sufficient to establish trust.
3. Synthesis innovation: comparison, taxonomy, framework, inferential lens, or cross-study pattern.
4. New insight or changed interpretation.
5. Evidence boundary: what the literature still cannot support.
6. Implication for research, education, policy, or practice.

Do not manufacture controversy when a scoping review supports only descriptive mapping. In that case, make the map, boundary, and future evidence needs concrete rather than claiming to overturn the field.

Avoid leading with database counts, checklist compliance, or publication dates. Use those details to support the trustworthiness of the synthesis.
