# Original research playbook

## Editorial purpose

Tell one scientific decision story. The editor should understand why the question matters, what limitation in prior evidence the study overcomes, why the design is suited to that gap, what the central finding is, and what changes in interpretation or next action.

## Required story elements

1. Important problem: specific enough to establish stakes without a generic global-burden opening.
2. Knowledge gap: the unresolved inference or decision, not merely “few studies exist.”
3. Design advantage: why the chosen design makes the central finding more credible or informative.
4. Central finding: one memorable result or coherent finding cluster.
5. Meaning: what the result changes in understanding, methods, or future decisions.
6. Journal relevance: the readers or current conversation that can use the advance.

## Contribution hierarchy

Prioritize discovery or inference over method inventory. Use methods to explain credibility. Prefer one central finding plus two or three supporting contributions over a catalogue of analyses.

## Common overclaims

- Association is not causation.
- Statistical mediation is not a proven biological mechanism.
- A proxy is not direct measurement of the construct it approximates.
- Significance in one subgroup and non-significance in another is not proof of interaction.
- Exploratory model importance is not an individualized intervention target.

When such risks exist, preserve the finding and calibrate the interpretation rather than burying the result in disclaimers.
