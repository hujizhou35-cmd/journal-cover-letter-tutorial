# Editorial iteration rubric v3.0

## Route models

### Original Research

> Stakes -> unresolved limitation -> study response -> empirical anchor -> synthesized finding -> editorial meaning -> journal fit

### Review/Synthesis

> Field problem -> synthesis intervention -> empirical anchor -> editorial thesis -> changed decision or research agenda -> journal fit -> calibrated boundary

### Bibliometrics

> Field-scale uncertainty -> bounded corpus -> mapping capability -> empirical anchor -> mapping thesis -> coordination or research consequence -> journal fit -> metric boundary

## Editorial-effect rubric

Score each dimension from 1 to 5:

1. **Empirical-anchor fidelity**: the central evidence is concrete, manuscript-traceable, and central.
2. **Editorial-meaning clarity**: the letter states what the anchor changes.
3. **Gap or orientation quality**: the unresolved inference, synthesis problem, or field-scale uncertainty is specific.
4. **Route reasoning**: the letter follows the selected route rather than the official article-type label.
5. **Method abstraction**: methods are translated into capability or credibility rather than listed.
6. **Selection and synthesis**: one finding cluster, thesis, or map-level conclusion is recoverable.
7. **Editorial consequence**: the changed decision, agenda, or coordination priority is singular and defensible.
8. **Journal specificity**: fit maps a verified criterion or readership need to the contribution.
9. **Omission discipline**: no detail survives unless it changes judgment, credibility, or boundary.
10. **Claim calibration**: language reaches the strongest supported rung without causal, priority, ranking, or forecasting overreach.
11. **Submission completeness**: exact official article type, branch-specific declarations, and contact details are correct.

A benchmark-convergence test passes when there are no high-impact differences on dimensions 1-9, no claim-strength violation on dimension 10, and no missing mandatory item on dimension 11.

## Route-specific failures

- Research fails when it reads as an abstract or method inventory.
- Review fails when it can only be summarized as “a comprehensive review of an important topic.”
- Bibliometrics fails when it is a catalogue of databases, software, rankings, clusters, or keywords without a field-level interpretation and decision consequence.

## Revision loop

Generate a first draft, audit it, and revise the transferable rule responsible for each failed dimension. Maximum: three draft-audit rounds and two compression rounds. Stop when hard gates pass and additional editing changes only taste, cadence, or wording.
