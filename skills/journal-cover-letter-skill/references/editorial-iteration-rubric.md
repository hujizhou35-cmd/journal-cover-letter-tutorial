# Editorial iteration rubric: v1.0

## The 1-5-1-1 model

Create one central problem, no more than five layered contributions, one bounded implication, and one reader-specific journal-fit claim.

Order contributions when applicable:

1. credibility of the evidence or design;
2. new organization, taxonomy, or empirical finding;
3. new explanatory concept or interpretation;
4. framework, tool, model, or actionable output;
5. practice or research implication and its boundary.

Each item should answer: What did the manuscript produce? Why does that output matter to editorial judgment?

## Revision loop

Generate a first draft, audit it, and revise only the failed dimensions. Maximum: three draft-audit rounds and two compression rounds. Stop when hard gates pass and additional editing changes style rather than decision value.

## Claim-strength ladder

Use the strongest rung the evidence supports:

- describes or maps;
- identifies an association or recurring pattern;
- supports an interpretation;
- strengthens causal inference;
- establishes causality or mechanism only with direct, adequate evidence.

Do not turn mediation into proven mechanism, within-subgroup significance into between-group difference, a proxy into direct biological measurement, or exploratory analysis into clinical guidance.

## Editor-read audit

The first substantive paragraph should identify the decision problem and why it matters. Methods demonstrate credibility; they should not dominate. Results should be selective and concrete. Declarations should be complete but should not dilute the scientific case.
