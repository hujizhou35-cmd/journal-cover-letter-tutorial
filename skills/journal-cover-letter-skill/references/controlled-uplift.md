# Controlled editorial uplift v3.0

## Purpose

Preserve the persuasive force of a strong human pitch while preventing scientific or metric overstatement.

## Three zones

### Zone A: facts

Titles, counts, sample or corpus characteristics, effect estimates, study design, dates, registrations, database coverage, citation indicators, and declarations receive no uplift.

### Zone B: interpretation

Use verbs such as `identifies`, `distinguishes`, `reveals`, `reframes`, `maps`, `integrates`, or `clarifies` when the analysis directly warrants them. Record the empirical anchor and weaken the verb if interpretation could be mistaken for fact.

### Zone C: editorial significance

Use the strongest reasonable statement about why the work changes a current scholarly decision. Significance cannot repair a weak or unverified factual claim.

## Uplift levels

- `0_MINIMAL`: neutral reporting; use when evidence is primarily descriptive.
- `1_CALIBRATED`: explicit novelty and consequence with restrained promotion; default.
- `2_ASSERTIVE`: stronger framing supported by convergent or unusually decisive evidence.

For bibliometrics, `2_ASSERTIVE` never licenses unsupported ranking, quality, causal, or forecasting claims.
