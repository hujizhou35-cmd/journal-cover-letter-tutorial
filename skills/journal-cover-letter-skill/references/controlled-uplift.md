# Controlled editorial uplift

## Purpose

Preserve the mild promotional force of a strong human pitch while preventing scientific overstatement.

## Three zones

### Zone A: scientific facts

Titles, counts, sample characteristics, effect estimates, study design, dates, registrations, and declarations receive no uplift. Copy only from verified sources.

### Zone B: interpretation

The study or synthesis may support verbs such as identifies, distinguishes, reveals, reframes, integrates, exposes, or clarifies when the underlying analysis directly warrants them. Record the evidence trace and downgrade the verb if a reasonable reader could mistake interpretation for fact.

### Zone C: editorial significance

Use the strongest reasonable statement about why the work matters to a current scholarly decision. Examples include advancing a methodological agenda, changing how a body of evidence should be interpreted, or defining a more defensible boundary for practice. Significance cannot repair a weak or unverified factual claim.

## Uplift levels

- `0_MINIMAL`: neutral reporting; use when evidence is primarily descriptive.
- `1_CALIBRATED`: explicit novelty and consequence with restrained promotion; default.
- `2_ASSERTIVE`: stronger editorial framing supported by convergent or unusually decisive evidence.

For each promotional sentence, record: source, zone, risk level, and decision (`KEEP`, `STRENGTHEN`, or `WEAKEN`). Avoid vague self-evaluation such as “important,” “novel,” or “highly relevant” without specifying what is new and why it changes judgment.
