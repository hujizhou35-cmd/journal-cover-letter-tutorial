# Evolution: from complete summary to editorial decision brief

This public history describes design lessons only. The source conversations and real submission materials are intentionally not published.

## v1.0: factual foundation and unified iteration

The first version solved a reliability problem. It read manuscript-related files, built a status-aware Fact Sheet, asked how a prior cover letter could be used, researched current journal information, and transformed a draft through a unified `1-5-1-1` structure.

Its strength was disciplined evidence handling. Its limitation was treating original research and reviews as if they needed the same persuasive architecture.

## v2.0: article-type routing and bounded loops

Comparison with a rights-cleared human-authored benchmark revealed that an accurate letter can still be too defensive. Original Research often needs a scientific decision story: problem, gap, design advantage, central finding, and meaning. A Review needs synthesis credibility and intellectual reorganization.

Version 2.0 therefore added separate routes, a persuasion-preservation gate, and bounded state loops. This corrected flat writing without relaxing causal, mechanistic, subgroup, proxy, or clinical boundaries.

## v2.1: thesis-driven Review and controlled uplift

The Review route still risked assembling correct components without stating what the synthesis changes. Version 2.1 introduced a deeper editor-facing sequence:

> field diagnosis → synthesis intervention → editorial thesis → changed decision or agenda → journal conversation → evidence boundary

It also formalized controlled uplift. Facts cannot be promoted; interpretations can be strengthened only when traceable; editorial significance can be assertive when it remains reasonable. Length became adaptive rather than governed by a universal one-page or word-count target.

## Benchmark, not gold standard

A human-authored letter may be selective, concrete, future-facing, and mildly promotional. It may also compress scientific distinctions too far or carry context-specific wording. The Skill therefore analyzes choices and tradeoffs while independently verifying every fact and claim. Expertise informs the writing model; it does not replace evidence.

## Stable design lesson

The project evolved from “summarize the manuscript correctly” to “help an editor make a fast, well-grounded screening decision.” The strongest general rule is:

> Use the most persuasive interpretation that remains fully traceable to the manuscript and current journal evidence.
