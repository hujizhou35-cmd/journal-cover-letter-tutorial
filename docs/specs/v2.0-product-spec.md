# Journal Cover Letter Skill — Product Specification v2.0

## 1. Version objective

Version 2.0 upgrades the skill in three areas:

1. route the workflow by manuscript article type rather than applying one editorial architecture to every submission;
2. preserve scientific accuracy while increasing editor-facing persuasion and narrative force;
3. replace the former single-pass iteration with bounded, state-based loops and explicit stop conditions.

The design benchmark is the comparison between two cover letters for the same original research manuscript:

- the expert-authored benchmark had a clearer scientific story, more concrete novelty, and stronger editorial persuasion;
- the v1.0-generated letter had better factual discipline, claim calibration, declaration completeness, and one-page efficiency;
- v2.0 must combine both strengths: **strong narrative + strict evidentiary boundaries**.

---

## 2. Locked product decisions

- **Runtime target:** Portable Agent Skill compatible with Codex and ChatGPT surfaces that support the Agent Skills format.
- **Domain scope:** Medicine and life sciences first, while remaining usable for other academic disciplines.
- **Human-control gate:** Mandatory user confirmation after manuscript fact extraction, article-type routing, and contribution/story ranking.
- **Previous-letter handling:** Always ask whether a prior cover letter exists and how it may be used: format only; format and tone; maximum wording preservation; or fact checking only.
- **Default deliverables:** Final text, submission-ready DOCX, concise factual/journal-compliance audit, article-type/story audit, and remaining author-confirmation items.
- **Length default:** One page, normally about 300–450 words unless the target journal explicitly requires otherwise.
- **Safety principle:** Never trade factual accuracy for persuasion.
- **Persuasion principle:** Never make accuracy an excuse for writing a flat, defensive, or editor-unfocused letter.

---

## 3. Product objective

Convert manuscript files and current journal evidence into a concise editor-facing submission argument.

The system must not merely summarize the manuscript. It must:

1. identify the manuscript's article type and submission pathway;
2. extract a verified manuscript fact sheet;
3. select the correct article-type narrative;
4. identify the single most persuasive editor-facing story;
5. rank genuine contributions or discoveries;
6. research current official journal requirements and readership;
7. draft with calibrated confidence;
8. run bounded revision loops until both scientific and persuasion gates pass;
9. output a submission-ready letter and audit record.

---

## 4. Article-type router

### 4.1 Required classification

Before drafting, classify the manuscript as one of the following:

- `ORIGINAL_RESEARCH`
- `REVIEW_SYNTHESIS`
- `OTHER_OR_UNRESOLVED`

The user must confirm the classification before drafting.

### 4.2 ORIGINAL_RESEARCH includes

- observational studies;
- randomized or non-randomized intervention studies;
- laboratory, translational, clinical, epidemiological, computational, genetic, and mixed-method research;
- brief reports containing original data.

### 4.3 REVIEW_SYNTHESIS includes

- systematic reviews;
- meta-analyses;
- scoping reviews;
- narrative or critical reviews;
- evidence syntheses and state-of-the-art reviews.

### 4.4 OTHER_OR_UNRESOLVED

Protocols, methods papers, guidelines, case reports, data/resource papers, editorials, and other formats use a conservative fallback workflow. The skill must not silently force them into a Research or Review template. It must identify the mismatch and request confirmation or apply an explicitly labeled fallback.

## 5. Research article playbook

### 5.1 Editorial purpose

For original research, the cover letter should present a **scientific decision story**, not a compressed abstract and not a methods inventory.

The default narrative is:

> **Important problem → precise knowledge gap → design advantage → central finding → changed interpretation or consequence → journal-reader relevance**

### 5.2 Required research story elements

A Research cover letter must contain:

1. **Problem:** Why the scientific or clinical problem matters.
2. **Gap:** What specific uncertainty remains unresolved.
3. **Design advantage:** Why this study can address the gap better than prior work.
4. **Central finding:** The one result or result cluster that carries the submission.
5. **Meaning:** What the result changes in understanding, inference, or next-step research.
6. **Journal fit:** Why this finding belongs before this journal's readership now.
7. **Declarations:** Originality, simultaneous-submission status, author approval, conflicts, funding, and any required disclosures.

### 5.3 Research contribution hierarchy

Rank contributions in the following order unless the manuscript supports another order:

1. **central discovery or answer;**
2. **design or evidence advantage that makes the answer credible;**
3. **mechanistic, subtype, or heterogeneity insight;**
4. **causal, predictive, translational, or methodological extension;**
5. **boundary or next-step implication.**

Methods are not independent contributions unless the method itself is novel. A list of techniques must be converted into an explanation of what each technique adds to inference.

### 5.4 Research drafting rules

- State the central scientific story within the first 120 words.
- Include no more than 2–4 result clusters.
- Prefer one coherent finding chain over exhaustive result coverage.
- Use specific quantitative results only when they materially increase credibility or distinctiveness.
- Explain method complementarity rather than enumerating every analysis.
- Do not describe subgroup differences unless interaction or heterogeneity evidence supports the comparison.
- Do not turn statistical mediation into a proven biological mechanism.
- Do not turn internal model performance into clinical readiness.
- Do not call public data alone “full reproducibility” when code, environment, or workflow is not openly available.
- Do not equate a proxy or index with direct measurement of the underlying biological construct.
- Do not foreground the manuscript's main limitation unless omission would materially mislead the editor.
- When a boundary is necessary, place it after the positive contribution and frame it as calibrated scope, not self-rejection.

### 5.5 Research paragraph architecture

Default one-page structure:

1. **Submission sentence:** title, article type, target journal.
2. **Problem-gap paragraph:** scientific importance and unresolved question.
3. **Design-finding paragraph:** design advantage and central result chain.
4. **Meaning-fit paragraph:** novelty, interpretation, readership relevance, and one calibrated boundary if needed.
5. **Declarations and signature block.**

## 6. Review article playbook

### 6.1 Editorial purpose

For a review, the cover letter should show why the field needs this synthesis and what intellectual value the review adds beyond collecting studies.

The default narrative is:

> **Field problem → evidence base → synthesis innovation → new framework or insight → evidence boundary → implications for research or practice**

### 6.2 Required review story elements

1. **Field problem:** fragmentation, inconsistency, conceptual confusion, or an outdated evidence base.
2. **Evidence credibility:** scope, search period, study base, appraisal, or synthesis method as relevant.
3. **Synthesis innovation:** taxonomy, conceptual model, framework, reinterpretation, or new cross-study pattern.
4. **Decision value:** what researchers, educators, clinicians, or policymakers can understand or do differently.
5. **Evidence boundary:** what the synthesis supports and what remains unresolved.
6. **Journal fit and declarations.**

### 6.3 Review contribution hierarchy

Default order:

1. evidence-base credibility;
2. new synthesis or cross-study pattern;
3. taxonomy, framework, or conceptual advance;
4. practical or research decision value;
5. explicit evidence boundary.

### 6.4 Review drafting rules

- Do not make search mechanics the main story.
- Do not treat adherence to routine reporting standards as novelty.
- Do not merely restate the abstract or table of contents.
- Distinguish “comprehensive evidence coverage” from “new intellectual synthesis.”
- State how the review differs from recent competing reviews.
- Preserve a clear positive/negative evidence boundary.

## 7. Persuasion calibration

### 7.1 Core principle

The target is not maximum caution or maximum promotion. The target is:

> **the strongest editor-facing statement that the evidence can support.**

### 7.2 Claim-strength ladder

Use verbs according to evidence strength:

- **Demonstrates / establishes:** reserve for designs and results that justify a strong conclusion.
- **Shows / identifies / reveals:** use for clear empirical findings without implying more than the design permits.
- **Provides evidence for / supports:** use for triangulated, causal-inference, or convergent evidence with residual limitations.
- **Suggests / is consistent with:** use for exploratory, indirect, proxy-based, or weakly identified findings.

The skill must revise both overclaiming and unnecessary hedging.

### 7.3 Persuasion-preservation rules

Every draft must contain:

- one explicit novelty sentence;
- one sentence explaining why the study or synthesis matters now;
- one journal-readership relevance sentence grounded in official scope or observed publication pattern;
- one central contribution that remains memorable after a 30–45 second read.

Avoid:

- generic praise of the journal;
- long lists of methods or findings;
- defensive limitation paragraphs;
- repeated “may,” “might,” “potentially,” or “could” when stronger supported language is available;
- unsupported superlatives such as “first,” “unique,” “definitive,” or “fully reproducible.”

### 7.4 Boundary placement

A necessary evidence boundary should normally be expressed in one compact clause or sentence after the main contribution.

Preferred pattern:

> “By using [indirect/proxy/design-limited approach], the study provides [specific supported value] while defining the next direct validation step.”

Avoid opening the letter with the manuscript's main weakness.

## 8. Bounded loop architecture

### 8.1 Feasibility

The skill may use loops, but they must be **bounded, state-based, and auditable**. It must not rely on an uncontrolled “repeat until perfect” instruction.

Each loop must have:

- an entry condition;
- a state record;
- a maximum iteration count;
- explicit pass/fail criteria;
- an escalation or stop rule.

### 8.2 State object

Maintain an internal working state containing at least:

```yaml
article_type:
submission_branch:
fact_status:
critical_unresolved_items:
ranked_contributions:
candidate_story_angles:
selected_story_angle:
hard_gate_failures:
persuasion_failures:
iteration_count:
stop_reason:
```

### 8.3 Loop A — Intake and fact loop

**Goal:** obtain a reliable fact sheet and correct article-type route.

Process:

1. identify file roles;
2. extract facts and declarations;
3. classify article type and submission branch;
4. identify missing or conflicting critical facts;
5. ask only questions that cannot be resolved from the files;
6. repeat until the user confirms or unresolved items are explicitly accepted.

**Stop condition:** article type, title, authorship, target journal, submission branch, central results/contributions, and required declarations are confirmed.

### 8.4 Loop B — Story-selection loop

**Goal:** choose the strongest supported editor-facing narrative.

For Research:

1. generate up to three candidate scientific stories;
2. score each for importance, novelty, evidentiary support, coherence, and journal fit;
3. reject stories that depend on unsupported causality, subgroup differences, or clinical readiness;
4. select one central story and one secondary support line.

For Review:

1. generate up to three candidate synthesis stories;
2. score each for evidence credibility, synthesis novelty, conceptual value, decision relevance, and journal fit;
3. select one central synthesis story.

**Stop condition:** one story clearly outranks the alternatives or the user selects among tied candidates.

### 8.5 Loop C — Draft, audit, targeted revision

Maximum: **three complete revision cycles**.

Each cycle:

1. draft or revise the letter;
2. run all hard audits;
3. run persuasion audits;
4. create a targeted revision plan;
5. revise only the failed dimensions;
6. re-audit.

Do not rewrite the entire letter blindly after every failure.

**Early stop:** all hard gates pass and no material persuasion failure remains.

**Failure stop:** after three cycles, output the best safe draft plus unresolved failures; do not label it submission-ready.

### 8.6 Loop D — Compression loop

Apply only after content and story are stable.

1. remove duplication;
2. merge low-value method details into design advantage;
3. retain the central story, strongest evidence, fit, and declarations;
4. verify one-page length and readability;
5. re-run fact and persuasion audits after compression.

Maximum: two compression passes.

### 8.7 Human-control points

Mandatory user confirmation:

1. after fact sheet, article-type classification, and ranked story/contribution proposal;
2. when critical facts remain unresolved;
3. before wording preservation from a prior letter;
4. when a high-risk claim is strategically important but evidentiary classification is debatable.

## 9. Audit gates

### 9.1 Hard gates

1. **Fact audit:** exact title, authors, article type, design, counts, dates, identifiers, findings, and declarations.
2. **Journal audit:** current scope, article type, readership, cover-letter rules, editor/salutation, and special pathway.
3. **Article-type audit:** the letter follows the correct Research or Review narrative.
4. **Claim-strength audit:** causality, subgroup comparison, mechanism, generalizability, prediction, implementation readiness, and superlatives.
5. **Declaration audit:** originality, prior publication/preprint, simultaneous submission, author approval, conflicts, funding, ethics, and required disclosures.

Any hard-gate failure blocks “submission-ready” status.

### 9.2 Quality gates

6. **Story audit:** one central story is identifiable and the paragraphs advance it.
7. **Contribution audit:** contributions are ranked, nonredundant, and decision-relevant.
8. **Persuasion audit:** novelty, importance, confidence, specificity, and journal relevance are sufficiently strong.
9. **Editorial-read audit:** problem, novelty, credibility, central finding or synthesis, fit, and declarations are clear within 30–45 seconds.
10. **Compression audit:** normally one page; no abstract duplication, method catalogue, or generic journal praise.

### 9.3 Research-specific checks

- subgroup difference claims require interaction or heterogeneity evidence;
- “mechanism” requires evidence beyond statistical mediation or association;
- “causal” must identify which exposure and outcome were causally tested;
- proxy/index constructs must not be presented as direct biological measurements;
- “clinical guidance,” “precision intervention,” or “implementation-ready” requires appropriate validation;
- “reproducible” must distinguish public data, public code, executable workflow, and independent replication.

---

## 10. Editorial operations

Apply the following operations within the revision loops:

- **Select:** choose the one story that should drive editorial screening.
- **Merge:** absorb secondary methods and analyses into a stronger design or evidence claim.
- **Promote:** elevate the central discovery, synthesis insight, and journal relevance.
- **Calibrate:** strengthen unnecessary hedging and weaken unsupported claims.
- **Demote:** move routine reporting, search mechanics, and secondary analyses out of the main narrative.
- **Delete:** remove generic praise, duplicated fit statements, obsolete submission language, defensive caveats, and low-value process detail.
- **Reorder:** for Research, present problem and gap before design and finding; for Review, present field problem before synthesis innovation.
- **Compress:** preserve scientific story and declarations while reducing length.

---

## 11. Core user journey

1. User uploads manuscript-related files.
2. Skill identifies file roles and extracts a manuscript fact sheet.
3. Skill classifies article type and submission pathway.
4. Skill proposes ranked contributions and candidate story angles.
5. Skill stops for user confirmation.
6. User confirms facts, article type, story direction, target journal, and optional prior-letter permissions.
7. Skill researches current official journal requirements, readership, scope, editorial information, special routes, and recent related content.
8. Skill enters the appropriate Research or Review drafting branch.
9. Skill runs the bounded draft-audit-revision and compression loops.
10. Skill outputs final text, DOCX, audit summary, story rationale, and unresolved confirmation items.

---

## 12. Source policy

Priority order:

1. official journal instructions for authors;
2. official aims and scope;
3. official article-type guidance;
4. official editorial-team page;
5. official publisher policy pages;
6. recent relevant content published by the journal;
7. recognized secondary sources only for noncritical gaps;
8. third-party summaries only as labeled fallbacks.

Internally label each journal-related finding as:

- explicit requirement;
- explicit scope statement;
- observed publication pattern;
- editorial inference;
- unverified claim.

Never call an inferred pattern an official preference.

Manuscript facts must come from the uploaded manuscript package or explicit author confirmation. Journal research must not overwrite manuscript facts.

---

## 13. Submission branches

Support:

- initial submission;
- revised/resubmitted manuscript to the same journal;
- transfer or special prior-review pathway;
- new submission to a different journal after rejection, withdrawal, or non-transfer.

The system must not reuse resubmission language for a new journal.

Article-type routing and submission-pathway routing are independent dimensions. For example, an original research article may be an initial submission, resubmission, or new-journal submission.

---

## 14. Output contract

Default outputs:

1. **Final cover-letter text**
2. **Submission-ready DOCX**
3. **Fact and declaration audit**
4. **Journal and article-type compliance audit**
5. **Story and persuasion audit**
6. **Remaining author-confirmation items**
7. **Optional concise rationale:** central story selected, alternatives rejected, and reasons

A letter may be labeled `SUBMISSION_READY` only when all hard gates pass.

## 16. MVP implementation priorities

### Required for v2.0

- Research versus Review article routing;
- Research scientific-story architecture;
- Review synthesis architecture;
- persuasion-preservation audit;
- subgroup-interaction, proxy/direct-measurement, mechanism, clinical-readiness, and reproducibility checks;
- bounded draft/revision loops with iteration counters and stop reasons;
- article-type-specific templates;
- final DOCX and audit outputs.

### Deferred

- persistent journal-policy database;
- graphical workflow wizard;
- automated submission-system entry;
- full visual style-diff engine;
- separate optimized branches for protocols, case reports, guidelines, methods papers, and resource papers;
- public plugin packaging, authentication, analytics, and hosted storage.

## 17. Validation plan

Validation must include at least:

1. one systematic or scoping review;
2. one narrative review;
3. one observational original research article;
4. one experimental or interventional original research article;
5. one manuscript with a prior cover letter;
6. one manuscript with high-risk causal or subgroup claims;
7. one manuscript with a proxy measure but no direct measurement of the claimed biological construct.

For each case, compare:

- article-type routing accuracy;
- factual error rate;
- claim-strength violations;
- editor-facing story clarity;
- persuasion score;
- one-page compliance;
- human reviewer preference;
- number of loop iterations and stop reason.

---

## 18. Acceptance criteria

Version 2.0 passes when:

- Research and Review manuscripts receive materially different, appropriate narrative structures;
- the Research branch produces a clear problem-gap-design-finding-meaning story;
- no hard factual or claim-strength violations remain;
- the final letter is not materially flatter or more defensive than a strong human-written benchmark;
- the central contribution is identifiable within 30–45 seconds;
- revision loops terminate deterministically;
- all loop decisions and unresolved items are auditable;
- final DOCX output is visually submission-ready.
